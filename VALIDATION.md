# Validation

How each counting method and building block has been checked, against what, and to what
precision. The aim throughout is to check against something independent of this project's
own engine: official counts of real elections, independent implementations of the same
method (OpenSTV, droop, CRAN packages, pyRCV2), or, where neither exists, hand-derived cases.
Tests named below are under `library/src/test/java`, fixtures under `library/src/test/resources/testdata/`.
The checks against other implementations rerun together (`tools/reference/implementations.py`),
and `tools/reference/implementations.json` records what each last reported.

**Official counts**: how closely each jurisdiction's counts are reproduced, from its ballots. How
the US ranked-choice counts differ in reading ballots (overvotes, skipped rankings, write-ins)
and in running (several defeated at once, stopping at a majority) is tabulated under
[Real elections](#real-elections), before Portland.
"Every round" means every candidate's votes in every official round, to the precision the
official count gives them. Details are in [Real elections](#real-elections). The table is
generated from the checkers' results (`tools/reference/results_page.py --validation write`), and
the build fails when it no longer matches them. To refresh every result, this table and the
results page (`tools/reference/results/real-elections.html`) together, save each checker's data
under one directory, a subdirectory per checker (each tool's README says what it needs), and run
`python3 tools/reference/refresh_results.py DATA_ROOT`; `--dry-run` shows what would run and
what would be skipped for want of data, and `--compare` reruns everything without writing and
lists every contest whose result would change. On 9 October 2026 a full `--compare` (every
checker, NSW included, the ACT's and Ireland's fixture tests too, each record converted afresh)
reproduced all 17 results files exactly, after that week's changes to the ballot readers, Meek
and Warren, Borda and the error handling.

Every one is cross-checked against the election's published or officially declared result,
not against figures derived from this engine (`ScottishRealElectionFixtureTest`,
`OrkneyNorthIslesFixtureTest`, `BadenochAndStrathspeyFullReplayTest` with its expected figures
in `highland-2022-badenoch-and-strathspey.expected.txt`, `ActOfficialDistributionTest`, `ActKurrajongFixtureTest`,
`Irish2002GeneralElectionFixtureTest`). North Isles is the one where the final seat is settled
by elimination rather than a surplus transfer. When several candidates reach quota in the same
round they're elected together, the largest surplus transferred first, as each official count
does.

**Every Scottish council ward** (`tools/reference/scotland`): all 1,070 regular ward elections
with ballots in scot-elex elect the declared councillors, 1,068 matched automatically and two
by hand (a ballot file that truncates surnames, and a ward where Wikipedia's result box is wrong
and Denis Mollison's recount of the ballots confirms ours). That took one fix. When a surplus
transfer takes a candidate to quota, the official count elects them there and then, and the
next surplus passes them by; this engine used to wait until the next round, still crediting
them with the next transfer. In North Lanarkshire's Coatbridge South in 2017 that was decisive:
Fergus MacGregor took 2.15 votes of Tom Castles's surplus he never had, and Jim Brooks finished
0.58 ahead of Gordon Encinias at stage 6 instead of 0.12 behind, electing the wrong councillor.
It also corrected two fixture tests that had recorded the old behaviour as official (Shetland
North 2017, Orkney Kirkwall East 2022): their official results elect Emma Macdonald on 379.04 and
David Dawson on 340.55, the figures they reached when elected.

**Every stage of the Scottish counts.** The winners are one thing; the stages check the count
itself. Two sources:

- **The councils' own Candidate Votes Per Stage Reports**, the counting system's report for
  each ward in 2022, every figure to 5 decimal places: all 40 collected (Falkirk's 9 wards,
  Scottish Borders' 11, Highland's 20) match at every stage, every candidate's votes and the
  non-transferable votes, exactly; 2,135 figures (`tools/reference/scotland/stage_report.py`).
  Two of them are fixtures (`ScottishStageReportFixtureTest`), beside Badenoch and Strathspey.
- **Wikipedia's result boxes**, which give every candidate's figure at every count for 1,043 of
  the wards (2007–2022), transcribed from the councils' declarations to whole votes or 1–2
  places (`tools/reference/scotland/stages.py`, allowing for rounding or truncation): 874 match
  at every count. 148 have no count with more than one figure off, the shape of a transcription
  slip; 21 have a count with more. Where there's an official source to settle it, the slip is
  Wikipedia's: every 2022 ward whose first preferences disagree (16 of them) has first
  preferences in the Electoral Management Board's results file that equal this count's, and
  Falkirk's wards 1, 4 and 8, three of the 21, match Falkirk's own reports exactly. The rest,
  mostly from 2012 and 2017, have no official stage report online to check (in a few, such as
  Angus's Arbroath East and Lunan in 2012, the box's later counts are a vote or so off for
  several candidates, which may be the box or the ballot file).

The Irish elections are a different kind of check. Dáil counts transfer surpluses by physically
drawing a random sample of the last parcel at full value, so there is no reproducible official
round-by-round record at ballot level. What is checked: every first-preference tally matches
the published result, the elected set matches the TDs actually returned, and the `scottish`
ruleset (the closest common deterministic method) run on the real ballots matches an independent
implementation of that method, droop, at every stage to droop's 5 decimal places.

Also checked against real data:

- **Meek's method**: MapLibre's 2025 Governing Board election (6 candidates, 5 seats, 92
  ballots, run on OpaVote), from the real ballots and OpaVote's published per-round keep
  factors — see [Meek and Warren](#meek-and-warren).
- **Schulze**: a real Debian General Resolution's published pairwise tally — see
  [Schulze](#schulze).
- **Borda (Dowdall)**: two constituencies of Nauru's 2019 election — see [Borda](#borda).

Each of the 136 rounds of the five ACT counts matches one of the official counts exactly:
the same exclusions in the same order, every candidate's votes, the votes exhausted and the
loss by fraction, to the last of the 6 decimal places. (The official sheets have more counts:
they record each value group of an exclusion separately.) Getting there took three changes to
the `act-hare-clark` count, each settled by the official figures:

- **Rounding per transfer step.** The Act rounds "BP x TV" down to 6 places per transfer, so a
  tally is a sum of each surplus's and each value group's rounded gain, and so are the votes
  exhausted. Rounding each candidate's running total instead left Kurrajong, Murrumbidgee and
  Yerrabi a few millionths off.
- **A surplus no paper can carry** (worth more than the transferable papers) is loss by
  fraction on the ACT's sheets: Brindabella's 1,428 of Joy Burch's 2,244. ERS97, as pyRCV2
  counts it, calls the same thing exhausted, which is what the other last-parcel rulesets keep.
- **The count stops when the last seat is filled**, even partway through an exclusion: Yerrabi's
  last count leaves James Milligan "partially excluded", his remaining papers untransferred.

Checking the exhausted votes and the loss also found two bugs in last-parcel transfers (`ers97`,
`ers76`, `act-hare-clark`, `hare-clark-qpq`):

- **Papers with no further preference** were moved to exhausted at full value. They stay with
  the elected candidate as part of the quota: Kurrajong's count 2 shows none of Barr's 49 such
  papers exhausted, and the ERS97 rulebook's stage 2 shows 0.59 non-transferable, not Smith's 11.
  Reported exhausted votes were too high (Kurrajong's by 1,647 by the end); tallies and winners
  weren't affected.
- **The transfer value** is a value per paper (surplus / transferable papers; the Act's
  "BP x TV"), not a fraction of each paper's own value. Applied as a fraction, a candidate
  elected on papers from an earlier surplus passed on only part of their surplus; and when the
  surplus exceeded what the transferable papers were worth, papers gained value, creating
  votes. Papers now move at the transfer value, or at their own value if that's less. Four of
  the five ACT electorates have such surpluses (Ginninderra's Elizabeth Kikkert, Murrumbidgee's
  Giulia Jones, ...): before the fix, rounds in each of them stopped matching the official
  counts, by up to 40 (Murrumbidgee) to 1,873 votes (Brindabella), though the same candidates
  were elected. On 40 random elections under
  each last-parcel ruleset, tallies after such surpluses changed and no winner did.

The rounding loss is checked against the officially published figures where they exist:
Highland's round 2 loses 0.0096 (Russell Jones's surplus of 174 at a truncated 0.13593 per
vote), ERS97's stages 2 and 4 the rulebook's 0.59, and every count of the five ACT electorates
the official loss by fraction.
On those 40 random elections, no ruleset ever reports a negative loss, which would mean votes
created.

**The Australian Senate** (`senate`): every state and territory in 2016, 2019, 2022 and 2025,
32 counts in all (`tools/reference/aec`), matches the AEC's official distribution of preferences
at every round: 1,771 rounds, each one an official count, every candidate's votes, the
exhausted votes and the loss by fraction to the vote, with the same senators elected in the
same order. 2016 was a double dissolution, 12 senators per state; NSW's count that year, 151
rounds over 4.5 million ballots, takes about 17 seconds. Tasmania 2016 and 2019 are test
fixtures (`AecSenateFixtureTest`); 2016 ends on s273(17), below.

Getting there took two corrections, both from the official counts:
- No bulk exclusion. The Act allows it (s273(13A)) only on narrower terms than the usual rule,
  and the AEC excluded one candidate at a time where the usual rule (pyRCV2's, adopted at first)
  would exclude several together: ACT 2019 excludes Cowton at count 9 and Angel at count 11. One
  at a time, ACT, SA and QLD 2019 all matched.
- The end of the count (`electAsTheyStand`). When an exclusion leaves as many continuing
  candidates as seats, they're elected on their votes as they stand, without the excluded
  candidate's papers moving (s273(18); NSW 2019 count 429, Davey and Faruqi); and the last seat
  between two continuing candidates goes to the one ahead, without excluding the other
  (s273(17); QLD 2019 count 288, Waters over Ketter; also Tasmania and SA in 2016, SA and WA in
  2022). Neither changed who was elected, only the
  final figures, and here possibly the order of election. Ireland's and Scotland's official
  counts transfer the last exclusion, so this is a Senate setting only.

pyRCV2's Senate counter (as its own AEC test configures it, in exact arithmetic, but without
bulk exclusion) checked 200 seeded random elections too (`tools/reference/pyrcv2/check.py
--preset senate`): all 198 it can count match in every round, every candidate's votes, the
exhausted votes and the loss by fraction (it crashes in its exclusion code on the other 2).
pyRCV2's value-group exclusion can leave the excluded candidate with fractional or even negative
votes, which this engine doesn't, so the loss it reports is then off by those. None of these
200 does that. One of them is a fixture (`testdata/aec/pyrcv2/r134`, election 134), where
surpluses go in order of election rather than largest first: C2's 20 votes before C5's 23.

Run on 1,000 (`--elections 1000`, about 5 minutes), 969 match in every round and pyRCV2
crashes on 26. Four differ only in the loss by fraction, each by the votes pyRCV2 leaves on an
excluded candidate: r545 is a fixture, where C5 ends on -2/5 and pyRCV2's loss is 22/5 against
this engine's 4 (`AecSenateFixtureTest` counts C5's votes as lost and then matches every
round); in r218, r306 and r616 the votes left are -105/152, -351/355 and 1483/5920. The last,
r305, matches in every round but elects C1 and C6 where this engine elects C1 and C2: with one
seat left, C2 and C6 tie on 8 votes, as they did at every earlier count. This engine elects one
of the two (s273(17)) and pyRCV2, which has no such rule, excludes one; both settle the tie by
declared order, standing in for the Act's lot, which picks C2 either way, to elect or to
exclude.

**NSW councils** (`nsw-lg`): every councillor count of the 2024 NSW local government elections
the NSW Electoral Commission published figures for, 227 in all (`tools/reference/nsw`). 212
match its full distribution of preferences at every round: every candidate's votes and the
votes lost (exhausted plus loss by fraction, which it reports together), to the vote. The other
15 reach a tie for exclusion that no earlier count settles, which the Regulation leaves to lot;
the Commission's draw and this project's declared order part ways there. All 227 elect the
councillors the Commission declared. (Balranald's published distribution has no counts in it.)
Six are test fixtures (`NswLocalGovernmentFixtureTest`).

The Commission's distributions show each parcel's sub-count, and the rules came from them:

- **The surplus fraction** is the surplus over the transferable votes: the candidate's votes
  less the value of the papers with no further preference, which stay with them (Newcastle ward
  4: 4,172 over 10,397). Each paper moves at its value times that fraction.
- **Each candidate's gain from each parcel**, the papers received in one transfer at one value,
  is rounded down to whole votes; an excluded candidate's parcels all move in one count, each
  rounded on its own. A new transfer method, `weighted-transferable`, does both.
- **Surpluses go in order of election**, two elected on equal votes in one transfer in the
  order the count before set (Upper Lachlan: Searl ahead of Reynolds). That last part was a bug
  for every ruleset: the tie-break looked back from the start of the stage, not from the
  transfer that elected them.
- **The count ends once the leaders can't be overtaken** (`electSureWinners`): the leading
  candidates for the seats left are elected as soon as the rest together, with any surplus
  still to transfer, have fewer votes than the last of them (Blayney: Dorsett on 568, under the
  quota of 569, against 554). A surplus that could still change it is transferred first (Ballina
  B: Bailey's 440 lets Ramsey overtake Broadley).
- **A candidate elected mayor** leaves the councillor count: their papers go to the next
  preference, and those ranking no one else don't count towards the quota (Coffs Harbour, quota
  5,021 rather than 5,022). The quota is now on the votes the first count gives a candidate,
  which changes nothing where no candidate has withdrawn.

OpenTally's NSW preset counts the same way (below), short of one shortcut: where an exclusion
would leave as many candidates as seats, it elects them without transferring the excluded
candidate's papers. No 2024 count reached that point without the leaders already being sure of
their seats.

- **Rounds.** Defeating several hopeless candidates at once passes through the same states as
  defeating them one at a time, and stopping at a majority doesn't change the winner; neither
  does an official round that changes nothing (New York's when a candidate first passes 50%,
  Alameda's 2012–2017 first pass dropping an empty write-in line). So these counts are checked by
  requiring every official round to be one of this count's rounds, in order, with the same
  candidates continuing; where the official count is one a round to two left, that is round for
  round.
- **Write-ins eliminated first.** New York's rule that the write-ins go before anyone else, even
  outpolling two candidates (its 2025 mayoral primary), is the one case where passing them over
  isn't only a convenience: counted as a candidate, they'd be defeated later and the rounds
  would differ. Passed over, every round but the first matches; the first is checked against the
  record's first choices.
- **Repeated candidates.** Counting a repeat once and passing over it are the same reading.
  Alaska's Dominion counts read a repeat as a skipped ranking, so that two in a row end the
  ballot; the reports' transfers show it. The same reading puts Maine's rounds off.
  Eastpointe's counts alone need a repeat to end the ballot.

**Portland** (`portland`): the four multi-seat City Council contests of 2024, Portland's first
(`tools/reference/portland`), from Multnomah County's cast vote records, against its official
round-by-round results (RCTab's tabulation summaries): at the official threshold, every round of
Districts 2 and 4 is one of this count's rounds, every candidate to 4 decimal places, and in

(`PortlandFixtureTest`).

The rules are City Code 2.08.030 C, and the ballots needed reading as the County reads them:

- **The transfer value is truncated twice**, the surplus fraction and then each ballot's value
  times it, to 4 places (`fractional-truncated`). With only the fraction truncated, District 4
  goes wrong from round 30.
- **Overvotes and write-in lines.** A ranking with two marks is skipped, and the numbered
  write-in lines count as marks for that, but alone are skipped, only "Uncertified Write In"
  being a candidate; every row of the cast vote record counts, those flagged as remade too.
  Read so, the first round matches the County's to the vote; any other reading doesn't.
- **Surpluses are transferred together**, a candidate they take to quota elected in the next
  round (`simultaneousSurpluses`). No 2024 count turned on it (the counts match with it off too),
  so it rests on the Code and a unit test; nor do they tell Portland's truncation from WIGM's,
  which also matches.

**Minneapolis** (`minneapolis`): the City's multi-seat contests of 2017, 2021 and 2025, the Park
and Recreation Board at large and the Board of Estimate and Taxation (`tools/reference/minneapolis`),
from its cast vote records, against its summary statements (those of 2025 marked "unofficial"):
at the official threshold, every official round of all six is one of this count's rounds, every
candidate to 4 decimal places, and the same candidates are elected. All six are fixtures
(`MinneapolisFixtureTest`). In every such round the statement's exhausted votes are this
count's too, as they are in every matched round of the 29 single-seat races below
(`rcvcheck.exhausted_line`).

The rules are **inferred**, partly. The ordinance as enacted in 2006 (167.70) is the only text
found; the current Code of Ordinances wasn't reachable. What the counts show:

- **Mathematical elimination comes before any surplus.** Each round, every candidate who can't
  win is defeated together, while surpluses wait: the lowest whose votes, with every surplus
  still to move, are less than the next candidate's (the 2006 text's 167.70(a)(1)d). In 2021 the
  undeclared write-ins go before Forney's surplus moves; in 2025 McKelvey goes while Forney is
  over the threshold.
- **So does a test the 2006 text doesn't have**: anyone ranked on too few ballots still in play
  to reach the candidate holding the last of the seats left is defeated too. Without it, 2017's
  first round keeps Honerbrink and Casserly and 2025's third keeps Dowgwillo; with it, every
  round matches. The two tests run side by side, neither seeing the other's defeats: 2025's
  third round defeats Schneider, whose 7,494 are less than Dowgwillo's 7,524, beside Dowgwillo,
  though together they pass McKelvey's 12,914; and 2017's first keeps Hogan, whom the first
  test would defeat once the second's were gone.
- **A candidate over the threshold keeps receiving ballots until their surplus moves** (Forney
  gains 17 in 2021's second round, 5,475 in 2025's fourth), and keeps the truncation remainder
  after it does (26,663.2470).
- **The transfer value is truncated twice**, to 4 places, as Portland's is (`fractional-truncated`).
- **Overvotes and skipped rankings are passed over**, which matches every first round; a
  candidate ranked again counts once.
- **One surplus a round, the largest**, as the 2006 text's steps repeat; no count had two
  waiting at once, so that part is untested.
- **The count ends as soon as enough candidates have reached the threshold to fill the seats**
  (167.70(a)(1)a), before anyone is defeated. The 2013 Board of Estimate count shows it: Becker
  and Wheeler both pass the threshold in the first round and are elected there. This count
  defeated the other candidates first, with the same winners, until that was fixed.

Four are fixtures (`MaineIrvFixtureTest`): the 2nd District's 2018 and 2022 races, the 2020
Senate District 11 primary, and the 2026 Senate District 4 primary.

**Eastpointe, Michigan** (`portland`; `tools/reference/eastpointe`): the City Council elections
of 2019 and 2021 (2 seats each), from Macomb County's cast vote records as FairVote's collection
has them, against the RCTab summaries of the County's counts published on RCVis: every round is
one of this count's rounds, every candidate to 4 decimal places, at the official threshold, with
the same councillors elected, and each round's inactive ballots are this count's exhausted votes.
The summaries' residual surplus is this count's rounding loss in 2019; in 2021 it is 0.0125 more
from round 3 on, a slip of the summary's own: it lists the first surplus's residual again among
Tonia Gladney's transfers, which already account for all of her 203.2125 votes, so that round's
figures add up to 3,803.0125 of 3,803 ballots. Both are fixtures (`EastpointeFixtureTest`).

- **Ballots, as RCTab was set.** A skipped ranking or a write-in is passed over, but two in a row
  end the ballot; an overvote ends it; and so does a candidate ranked again. That last setting
  is what decides the transfers: 6 ballots ranking DeMonaco twice before their next choice
  exhaust in the 2021 summary when his surplus moves. Read so, both first rounds match to the
  vote.
- **The summary's own slip.** The 2021 summary's round 4 has Rob Baker on 1,058.8175: 2 votes
  more than its round 3 tally plus the transfers it lists for that round, and more than the
  total it carries into round 5. This count has the consistent 1,056.8175; every other figure of
  that round, and every later round, match.
- **What can't be told apart.** The only surpluses moved are of ballots still worth 1, where
  `fractional`, `wigm` and `fractional-truncated` all agree; the `portland` ruleset, another
  RCTab count at 4 places, is used. Each summary's last round, moving a surplus after both seats
  are filled, isn't a round here.

**Albany, California** (`albany`): the City Council and Board of Education contests of 2022 and
2024 (`tools/reference/albany`), from Alameda County's cast vote records as FairVote's collection
has them, against the County's Dominion "RCV Detailed Reports": every official round of all four
is one of this count's rounds, every candidate to the report's decimal places, at the official
first threshold, and the same candidates are elected. In every round the votes no longer with a
candidate are this count's too, to the report's decimal places: its exhausted votes and
overvotes, less the first round's (a ballot whose first ranking is an overvote never comes into
this count), are this count's exhausted votes, and its rounding remainders ("Remainder Points",
"Surplus Transfer Remainder") are this count's rounding loss. All four are fixtures
(`AlbanyFixtureTest`).

The reports name their settings, and the counts confirm them:

- **2024**: a threshold fixed on the first round's continuing ballots, 4 decimal places, the
  surplus fraction and each ballot's new value truncated (`fractional-truncated`, as Portland's),
  surpluses largest first (McQuaid's 158 before Hansen-Romero's 78), and the truncation
  remainder set aside, the elected candidate left on exactly the threshold.
- **2022**: the threshold recomputed every round on the votes the candidates then hold
  (`quotaEachRound`), and 6 places. The Council count turns on it: Miki's surplus is 521, over a
  threshold fallen from 2,387 to 2,355, not 489. That it's recomputed between two surpluses of
  one round, too, as the report has it for the Board of Education, no count shows: that count
  ends before the second moves.
- **Overvotes end the ballot** (Dominion's "skip overvoted rankings" is off); skipped rankings
  and unqualified write-ins are passed over. So every candidate's first round matches to the
  vote; one ballot the County calls blank is an overvote here, which counts for nobody either way.

Minneapolis's 2009 and 2013 contests, published as spreadsheets
of a different shape (2009 lists every write-in by name), aren't checked.

## The ERS97 model election

The Electoral Reform Society's worked "model election" from the ERS97 rulebook (11 candidates,
6 seats, 753 votes), with ballots and expected figures from an independent STV
implementation's own correctness test ([pyRCV2](https://yingtongli.me/git/pyRCV2))
(`Ers97ModelElectionFixtureTest`). It caught two real bugs:

- **Exclusion by value.** A single-shot elimination credited a candidate with 108.89 votes at
  the point they crossed quota partway through an elimination, where the rulebook's count
  sheet shows 108.68. Last-parcel rulesets now transfer an excluded candidate's ballots one
  value group at a time, largest first, and a candidate who reaches quota partway through
  stops receiving the rest.
- **Bulk exclusion.** ERS97 rule 5.2.5 excludes several of the lowest candidates together
  whenever their combined votes, plus any pending surplus, still couldn't reach the candidate
  above them. Excluding Wright and Glazier one at a time instead turned the rulebook's winner,
  Vicar, into a wrong Baron; [OpenSTV](http://www.openstv.org/) batches the two, as the rulebook
  does. The `batchExclusion` ruleset flag takes the largest such set that leaves enough
  candidates for the seats and doesn't split candidates on equal votes, as pyRCV2 does. Enabled
  for the `ers97`/`ers76` presets only: the Scottish statute
  ([SSI 2007/42 rule 50](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2007/42)) only ever excludes one
  candidate, and for Hare-Clark no primary source settles it either way.

The count now replays the rulebook's count sheet in full: every round matches one of its
stages, every candidate's votes and the non-transferable votes, through the last stage. That
took two more of its provisions, as pyRCV2 counts them (`deferSurpluses` and `votesRequired`,
again only in the `ers97`/`ers76` presets), and one change for every last-parcel ruleset:

- **Surplus deferral.** Pending surpluses wait while exclusions go ahead, unless together they
  could change which candidate, or bulk exclusion, comes next. Duke's 1.10 waits to the end.
- **Votes required for election.** The total active vote divided by one more than the seats
  left, rounded up: as votes are lost it drops below the quota, and Prince (104.31) and Freeman
  (101.62) are elected on it. It's worked out again after each election, so one step can elect
  several candidates.
- **The count ends when the last seat is filled**, with any surplus untransferred.

pyRCV2 itself runs as a Python program (`tools/reference/pyrcv2`), so it also served as a
reference on 100 seeded random elections (4–13 candidates, up to 1,000 voters;
`tools/reference/pyrcv2/check.py --preset ers97`): 98 match it in every round. In the other 2
every round matches too, but pyRCV2 elects a candidate more than there are seats: two
candidates finish tied exactly on the votes required for the last seat (r67's C2 and C4 on
26.00, r100's C2 and C3 on 255.00), and it elects both. Getting there meant fixing pyRCV2 on
the way: it records each stage without copying it, so its tie-break, which looks back to the
last stage where the tied candidates differed, only ever saw the current votes and fell
through to its last resort. (An earlier version of this page put one difference down to how
this engine looks back for ties; it was that bug.) Three of the 100 are fixtures
(`testdata/ers97/pyrcv2/r20`, `r36` and `r86`), picked for what they exercise: a surplus still
waiting when the last seat is filled, two candidates elected in one step as the votes required
falls, and a deferred surplus with two candidates excluded together.

The same 100 elections check the inclusive rulesets against pyRCV2's weighted inclusive
Gregory counter:

- **Exact fractional transfer** (the default `stv`): all 100 match in every round, to the 10
  places this project reports exact values to.
- **`scottish`**: all 100 match within a hundredth of a vote, 63 exactly. The rest is
  rounding: when a surplus comes from ballots of different values, pyRCV2
  truncates one transfer value and scales each ballot by it, where the Scottish rules give each
  value its own truncated transfer value. Highland's official count sheet settles which is
  right: after Muriel Cockburn's surplus it shows Bill Lobban on 978.40465, as this engine
  counts; pyRCV2's way gives 978.40485.
- Five of them first differed in their last round: when one stage's surpluses filled the last
  seats, this engine still transferred the first surplus before recording the others'
  elections. pyRCV2 stops there, and so do official Scottish counts (Shetland North 2017 ends
  at stage 2, Orkney Kirkwall East 2022 likewise), so now this engine does too, under every
  ruleset: once the last seat is filled, no further surplus moves.

## OpenTally

[OpenTally](https://yingtongli.me/git/OpenTally) (revision 3d4c415, still its latest in October
2026) is an independent STV counter with presets for many real rule sets, each option documented
against its legislation. `tools/reference/opentally` counts the same 200 seeded random elections
(5–12 candidates, 1–5 seats, 30–600 voters) with OpenTally and with this project, preset by
preset, and matches every round to an OpenTally stage: every candidate's votes, and the votes
set aside (exhausted plus lost) to within 10⁻⁹. OpenTally breaks a tie it can't settle backwards
at random, where this project uses declared order, so elections that differ after such a tie are
counted apart.

| This project | OpenTally | Every round matches | After a random tie | Otherwise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| default `stv` (exact Gregory) | weighted inclusive Gregory, rational | 192 | 8 | 0 |
| `scottish` | "Scottish STV" | 173 | 8 | 19, all within 0.001 |
| `act-hare-clark` | "Australian Capital Territory STV" | 188 | 9 | 3, set-aside only |
| `senate` (less `electAsTheyStand`) | "Australian Senate STV", without early election | 179 | 13 | 8, set-aside only |
| `hare-clark-qpq` (10× the voters) | Tasmania's Schedule 4, from OpenTally's options | 189 | 1 | 10, set-aside only |
| `nsw-lg` | "NSW Local Government STV" | 180 | 13 | 7, OpenTally's end-of-count shortcut |
| `vic-lc` (less `electAsTheyStand`) | "Victorian Legislative Council STV", without early election | 180 | 13 | 7, set-aside only |
| `wa-lc` (less `electAsTheyStand`) | "Western Australia STV", without early election | 161 | 16 | 23, set-aside only |
| `ers97` | "ERS97" | 163 | 11 | 26, OpenTally's own exceptions |
| `meek` | "Meek STV (2006)" and "(New Zealand)" | the same winners in all 200 | | |

The differences that remain are understood:

- **Set-aside only** (`act-hare-clark`, `senate`, `hare-clark-qpq`, `vic-lc`, `wa-lc`): every candidate's votes
  match in every round. OpenTally's exclusion by transfer value leaves the excluded candidate
  holding fractional or even negative votes (C5 on −1.08 in one Senate election), as pyRCV2's
  does, so the totals it sets aside differ by those; and it doesn't round exhausted votes to the
  ACT's 6 places, which the official ACT counts do. This engine matches the AEC's and Elections
  ACT's official counts on both.
- **`scottish`**: fifth-decimal rounding of a surplus from ballots of different values, as with
  pyRCV2 above; Highland's official count sheet confirms this engine's.
- **`ers97`**: pyRCV2 agrees with this engine on all 26. OpenTally documents these as its own
  departures from ERS97: it doesn't defer a surplus exactly equal to the gap between the two
  lowest candidates ([E8]), and it defers for bulk exclusion differently ([E9]).
- **Meek**: OpenTally's Meek presets stop iterating as soon as the surplus left can't change an
  exclusion, and stop at their own tolerance, so the figures between exclusions aren't
  comparable; the winners are.
- **`nsw-lg`**: in all 7, OpenTally ends on a tie for the last seat by electing the other
  candidates without transferring the excluded one's papers; this engine transfers them first.
  The same councillors are elected (see [NSW councils](#real-elections)).
- **The Senate's end of count**: OpenTally also elects candidates who can no longer be
  overtaken, which the Act doesn't provide for (ConcreteSTV's reading of s273 agrees); with that
  off on both sides the counts are compared up to the end, which the AEC's 32 official counts
  check above.

It found four bugs, each now fixed:

- **ACT surplus order.** Schedule 4 clause 7 of the ACT's Act deals with the surplus of whoever
  reached quota at the earliest count first, the largest of several from the same count; this
  engine took the largest pending surplus. None of the 2020 counts had a larger surplus waiting
  behind an earlier one. `act-hare-clark` now has `surplusOrder: election`.
- **ERS97's votes required** was rounded down, not up: "rounding up" truncated the negated
  share, and truncation goes towards zero. With 29.59 active votes for 2 seats the votes required
  is 9.87, not 9.86, so a candidate on 9.86 must wait for the next surplus. The 100 random
  elections against pyRCV2 never hit it; `testdata/ers97/pyrcv2/ot11` is the one that did.
- **`hare-clark-qpq` didn't follow Tasmania's Act.** It divided a surplus over the papers with
  a next preference and truncated the transfer value to 2 places. Schedule 4 of the Electoral
  Act 2004 (Tas) divides it over every paper of the last parcel and counts each candidate's
  share in whole votes, and the Tasmanian Electoral Commission truncates the value to 6 places
  (its 2 places are for council elections); surpluses go in order of election. A new transfer
  method, `unweighted-exclusive:6`, does that.
- **A tie for exclusion looked back only to whole rounds.** Where an exclusion moves in groups
  (by transfer value, or parcel by parcel), each group is a count of its own (the AEC numbers
  them so, and Victoria's s114A(28) says so), and "the last count at which they differed" can
  fall partway through one. In one random Victorian election C4 and C7 tie on 37; C7 had 36 to
  C4's 37 midway through the previous exclusion, so C7 goes, as OpenTally has it, where this
  engine took the round before (C4 30, C7 32) and excluded C4. It changes no real count checked
  here, nor any ERS97 election against pyRCV2.

`vic-lc` and `wa-lc` come from the Acts (Victoria's s114A; WA's Schedule 1) and are checked only
against OpenTally: neither Commission publishes the Legislative Council's ballot papers (WA
publishes every count's calculation steps). Four of the random elections are fixtures
(`OpenTallyFixtureTest`), picked for the exclusion orders and the tie above. OpenTally lists its
own WA preset as not validated, and WA's Schedule 1 groups an excluded candidate's papers by the
transfer they came in, where OpenTally (and so this engine) splits them by the parcel they came
from within it.

OpenTally's CSV report can't show Meek's stages, so the tool reads its text report. It crashes
on some single-seat elections in a log message, which the tool reports apart.

## Classic STV building blocks

- **Quotas.** Droop is exercised by every real election above. Hare is checked against the CRAN
  package [`STV`](https://cran.r-project.org/package=STV) (Emerson et al.), which implements
  the same unrounded `votes / seats` (`quotaMethod = "Hare", quotaFloor = FALSE`): two counts,
  one landing exactly on quota and one with a fractional surplus transfer, match its output
  round by round (`StvCounterTest`). OpenSTV's Hare quota is dead code (no method plugin
  selects it) and adds a "+1" that isn't part of the textbook formula. Hagenbach-Bischoff runs
  Wikipedia's "Counting single transferable votes" worked example, whose own working uses the
  unrounded 57/(2+1) = 19 (`HagenbachBischoffQuotaFixtureTest`). Imperiali has no
  independent implementation; a hand-derived case covers the edge it's prone to — three
  candidates reaching its smaller quota in the same round for two seats.
- **Transfer methods and rounding.** WIGM and truncation are exercised by the Scottish real
  elections and Badenoch and Strathspey's full replay; last-parcel by the ERS97 model election;
  last-parcel with deferred rounding by the five ACT electorates.
- **Split tied rankings** (fractional transfer). Checked against CRAN's
  [`vote`](https://cran.r-project.org/package=vote) package (Sevcikova, Silverman, Raftery),
  read at source level: when one of several tied candidates is elected only their own share is
  scaled by the surplus ratio, and when one is eliminated whoever remains at that rank inherits
  the *whole* pool next round, not just their own share. `StvCounterTest` has a hand-derived
  case for that inheritance.
- **Truncate and skip tied rankings.** Cross-checked against OpenSTV's own overvote cleaning
  ("San Francisco" for `truncate`, "Cambridge" for `skip`) run through its `ScottishSTV`
  method, on four synthetic ballot sets with hundreds of tied rankings: quota, winners and every
  round's tallies match exactly (`TiedRankingPolicyTest`, plus a `StvCounterTest` case where
  the two policies elect different winners). The bundled presets' choice of `truncate` comes
  from each rulebook's text — see the [README](README.md#bundled-rulesets).

## Meek and Warren

`MeekCounter` follows droop's Meek rule ([jklundell/droop](https://github.com/jklundell/droop)
at revision cf58293, May 2020, still its latest in October 2026; `meek arithmetic=fixed
defeat_batch=none`) in the same fixed-point arithmetic, and droop's
`warren` rule for Warren's method.

- **Against droop.** 144 elections (MapLibre plus 143 synthetic: the four tied-ballot sets
  above as truncated, skipped and split, 120 random, 7 hand-built covering exclusions decided by
  tie-break, iteration stopped by a stalled surplus, and counts ending by electing the
  remaining candidates): every round's tallies, residual and surplus match droop's report to
  the last decimal place, for Meek and for Warren, at the default 9 places and at 6, 12 and 13
  (`--precision`). Warren's figures differ from Meek's in 73 of the 120 random elections and its
  winners in 7, so the Warren check isn't vacuous. `MeekDroopReferenceTest` replays six stored
  droop reports; `MeekCounterTest` has small droop-confirmed cases, including one where Warren
  and Meek differ.
- **Against droop, repeatably** (`tools/reference/droop`): the 144 above weren't kept as a tool,
  so this one replaces them as the check anyone can rerun. On 300 seeded random elections (3–10
  candidates, 1–5 seats, a withdrawn candidate in a quarter, up to 2,000 voters a ballot line),
  counted by Meek and by Warren at 9, 6, 12 and 13 places: every one of the 11,518 rounds matches
  droop's report exactly, tallies, residual and surplus, and every election elects the same.
  Warren's figures differ from Meek's in 138 of the elections and its winners in 3. Two things
  aren't compared as they stand. droop leaves ballots that rank only withdrawn candidates out of
  the count, where this project counts them exhausted from the start; either way they're out of
  the quota, Meek's being over the active votes, so the checker subtracts them from this
  project's exhausted votes. And droop stops on one count of the 2,400, the 265th election's Meek
  count at 6 places (below). A quota planted without droop's last-place epsilon fails all 100
  elections tried.
- **A surplus rounded below zero** (fixed 25 October 2026): rounding can leave an elected
  candidate a hair under the quota, so that the elected candidates' votes less the quota, summed
  as droop sums them, fall below zero. "Every hopeful within the surplus of the lowest" then took
  in none, not even the lowest, and the count stopped on an empty tie ("tied must not be empty"),
  as droop does (its `IndexError`). Nothing is left to transfer there, so the lowest is excluded,
  tied only with candidates on exactly its vote. The 265th election, at 6 places, now elects the
  same four as at every other precision, and droop with that one line patched matches it every
  round, for Meek and for Warren, at all four precisions (`MeekCounterTest`).
- **Tied rankings under `split`** deliberately differ from droop in one place: droop sends any
  ballot containing a tie down a separate path that stops at the first rank with no standing
  candidate — tied or not — losing the rest of the ballot, while its path for untied ballots
  carries on. A copy of droop with just that path patched to carry on matches every round of
  the four tied-ballot elections and of 60 random ones.
- **MapLibre 2025** (`MeekMapLibreGoverningBoard2025FixtureTest`): the winners, the election
  order, and the round-1 tallies match OpaVote's published results PDF exactly, and every
  figure matches droop. This fixture caught a real bug: the first implementation converged
  the elected candidates' keep factors fully before checking whether anyone else had reached
  quota, giving the right winners but visibly wrong intermediate keep factors against OpaVote's
  published figures. Checking for new winners on every iteration, as droop also does, fixed it.
  OpaVote's own arithmetic isn't published, so its keep factors agree only to 5–6 significant
  digits.
- **OpenTally's Meek presets** ("Meek STV (2006)" and "(New Zealand)") elect the same candidates
  in all 200 of its random elections (see [OpenTally](#opentally)).
- **OpenSTV's `MeekSTV`** agrees on winners, but its round-by-round figures aren't comparable:
  it batch-eliminates and reports single keep-factor iterations as rounds.

## Schulze

Wikipedia's worked 5-candidate example; hand-derived cases for a genuine pairwise cycle (A
beats B beats C beats A, resolved to A>B>C by strongest paths), a perfect dead heat, a tied
ranking and an unranked candidate (`SchulzeMethodTest`); and a real Debian General Resolution's
published pairwise tally (`SchulzeDebianGr2004002FixtureTest`). Debian ballots are secret, so
that fixture checks the strongest-path computation and ranking against the real matrix rather
than replaying ballots. No real election with a published genuine cycle was found, so the cycle
case is hand-derived.

Against OpenSTV 1.7's Condorcet count (`tools/reference/openstv`), whose default completion,
Schwartz sequential dropping, is the beatpath method on winning votes: 500 seeded random
elections of 3-8 candidates, weighted ballots, rankings cut short, a withdrawn candidate in some,
small enough that 49 have a pairwise cycle and 12 of those end in a tie. Every pairwise figure,
every strongest-path strength and the winners match; where the dropping leaves several
candidates, they are exactly this project's first place.

That check found a bug, fixed on 10 October 2026. The ranking was by how many others each
candidate beats on strongest paths, on the claim that the relation is a total preorder. Schulze
proves it transitive, not that much: a candidate nobody beats can beat fewer others than another
candidate nobody beats, and so came below first place, though both are Schulze winners. 7 of the
500 elections showed it. First place is now every candidate nobody beats, and each later place
every candidate beaten only by those above; `SchulzeMethodTest` has a three-candidate case
(A ties B, B ties C, A beats C) that the old ranking fails. On Tideman's and the Pirate Party
elections in Schulze's archive the ranking changed in 11 of 70. Schulze STV uses it only to
break ties, and none of its committees, potential winners or supports changed; the proportional
ranking's effect is under [Schulze proportional ranking](#schulze-proportional-ranking).

OpenSTV's ballots can't hold tied rankings, so the tie rules are checked against pref_voting
1.18.2 (`tools/reference/pref_voting`), whose profiles take them: 500 more random elections, each
rank tied with the next three times in ten, rankings cut short, a withdrawn candidate in some (497
have a tie). Every pairwise figure matches pref_voting's supports under its extended strict
preference (ranked over unranked, unranked tied), the whole beat-path defeat relation on winning
votes matches the one this project's strongest paths give, and the winners match its first
place. The jar from before the fix above fails 2 of them, on the winners.

The pairwise matrix is counted in `long`s when the counts fit, and in `BigInteger`s when they
don't. No fixture or external check reaches the second way, since no real election is big
enough. `ScaledCountsTest` counts 200 random elections, with tied and truncated rankings, both
as they are and with every ballot's count ×10¹⁹. It requires every pairwise figure and path
strength to be exactly 10¹⁹ times the original, and the same ranking. A tie counted as a win in
either way makes it fail.

## Borda

Two constituencies of Nauru's 2019 parliamentary election, Anabar and Boe
(`BordaNauru2019AnabarFixtureTest`, `BordaNauru2019BoeFixtureTest`): per-voter ballots aren't
public, but Dowdall scoring depends only on each candidate's own (rank, count) totals, so a
ballot set rebuilt to match each published table must reproduce the official scores — and does,
exactly. Plus hand-derived cases for modified Borda, partial ballots, a three-way tie, a single
candidate, a blank ballot and a withdrawn candidate, who is passed over so that those after them
move up (`BordaMethodTest`).

Against OpenSTV 1.7's Borda count without ballot completion (`tools/reference/openstv`): the
same 500 random elections as Schulze's, every candidate's score equal. Both give n-1 points for
a first preference down to 0, none to an unranked candidate, and count n without the withdrawn.
OpenSTV has no tied ranks; pref_voting 1.18.2 (`tools/reference/pref_voting`) checks that rule.
On 500 random elections whose ballots rank every candidate, with ties, every score equals
(pref_voting's symmetric Borda score + N(n-1))/2: on such a ballot a candidate tied with k-1
others after p candidates scores the average of positions p to p+k-1, n-1-p-(k-1)/2, and the
symmetric score is n-p-k-p. A planted fault, a tied candidate scoring the group's first position,
fails all 500. A ballot both tied and cut short isn't compared: pref_voting gives unranked
candidates a share of the last places, where this project and OpenSTV give none; hand-derived
cases cover it.

## CPO-STV

CPO-STV has never been used in a public election, so there is no real data. Checked against:

- **Hand-derived cases** (`CpoStvCounterTest`), some found with a small independent Python
  implementation: Wikipedia's worked example (5 candidates, 3 seats); WIGM and fractional
  transfer agreeing; exhausted ballots; a four-way tie between outcomes; a three-outcome cycle
  that the beatpath completion still resolves; the `seats = candidates - 1` case where one shared
  candidate's surplus pushes another over quota; and equal surpluses transferring in declared
  order (this last one fixed a real bug: the order used to come from the outcome sets'
  iteration order, which varies between JVM runs — the same election scored differently on 5
  of 10 runs).
- **An independent implementation** (`tools/reference/cpo_stv.py`, written from the method's
  rules alone) on a realistic-size synthetic count: 8 candidates, 4 seats, 10,000 voters
  (`CpoStvNearTieCycleFixtureTest`). All 4,830 pairwise scores match exactly, and an
  independent Schulze computation over them picks the same committee. Committees differing by
  one equally popular candidate form a cycle there, and the beatpath completion elects a
  committee that loses head to head to the one every classic STV variant elects — a genuine
  difference between the methods, correct under CPO-STV's rules.
- **STVPoll** (`tools/reference/stvpoll`), VoteIT's Python library, version 0.5.6: the one
  implementation by someone else found that counts a comparison as Tideman does. On 300 seeded
  random elections (4–7 candidates, 2–4 seats), every one of the 23,140 comparisons whose two
  outcomes share at most one candidate has the same score difference, given STVPoll exact
  rounding (it rounds each ballot's value to 5 places) and the same Droop quota. With two or more
  shared candidates the rules differ: this project transfers the largest pending surplus until
  none is left, STVPoll gives each shared candidate one turn, in its set's order, and then
  removes them; 7,587 of those 26,327 comparisons differ. Of the 264 elections where STVPoll finds
  an outcome that beats every other, it's this project's winner in 253; in the other 11, it loses
  or ties in this count only against outcomes sharing two or three of its candidates. A copy of
  the check given a quota one vote higher fails 42 of 60 elections.

That check found a bug, fixed on 25 October 2026, the one the Schulze method had: outcomes were
ranked by how many others each beats on the beatpath, which can put an outcome nobody beats below
first. In 2 of the 300 elections the winners missed a second outcome nobody beats ({C2, C3} and
{C3, C5} in the 61st, `CpoStvCounterTest`); the ranking below first changed in 67, and no score.
First place is now every outcome nobody beats, and each later place every outcome beaten only by
those above.

Condorcet PHP (julien-boudry/Condorcet) has a CPO-STV too, but a variant of its own: candidates
at quota on first preferences are fixed in every outcome before any comparison, shared surpluses
move once, together, in floating point, and the completion is Schulze on margins. It isn't used
as a check.

## Schulze STV

Schulze STV has never been used in a public election either, but its author published a
program and its output for 66 elections from Nicolaus Tideman's database (`schulze3.zip`,
http://m-schulze.9mail.de/schulze3.zip: `singl01g.cpp` and an `A*_stv.dat` file of supports per
election). Checked against them:

- **Every support.** In all 65 elections within the committee cap, all 315,112 supports `N[committee; outsider]`
  match the published values to the 6 decimals they're given in. That covers proportional
  completion (most of these ballots are truncated, and a few rank candidates equally) and the
  closed-form support. A90 (20 candidates, 12 seats, 125,970 committees) is over the cap;
  Schulze's program didn't finish it in 10 minutes either.
- **The paper's worked example**, A53 (section 8.2): every support, and the winning committee {a,
  d, g, j} (`SchulzeStvTideman53FixtureTest`, which checks all 1,260 supports from the published
  file).
- **Winners.** The count follows the paper (arXiv:1804.02973, section 8.1.3). The 2017 program
  differs from it in two places, so it isn't a reference for every winner: it compares links by
  how a candidate ranks within its contest rather than by margin, and its Stage 4 tie-break
  uses an older single-winner ranking ("TBRL"). The committees agree on 63 of the 65 elections.
  On A04 the link definitions disagree. On A34, five committees go unbeaten and the two
  single-winner rankings break the tie differently. An independent count written from the paper alone
  (`tools/reference/schulze_stv/schulze_stv.py`) agrees with this one on every election with
  at most 500 committees (58, including A04 and A34): all 48,233 supports, the potential
  winners and the winners. Five of the elections need Stage 4.
- **Hand-derived cases** (`SchulzeStvCounterTest`): the closed form; two opposed factions
  winning a seat each rather than the compromise candidate; the paper's own proportional
  completion example (56/44); completion with nobody to copy, where every order of the tied
  candidates is equally likely (a deliberately wrong equal split over above/below patterns
  fails it); and a Stage 4 tie-break.

`tools/reference/schulze_stv` reruns all of this: the conversion, the comparison with the
published supports and with Schulze's program (with the one fix it needs to build), and the
independent count.

Two conventions had to be settled from the program, not the paper. Completion is done per
contest, over its M+1 candidates only. In the database's letter format, a candidate named twice
on a ballot ties everyone ranked since their first mention with them. Reading those ballots any
other way changes the supports of the 7 elections that have them.

Ballots are grouped in `long` counts when the total fits, and in `BigInteger` counts when it
doesn't, which no real election reaches. `ScaledCountsTest` counts 100 random elections (3,845
supports) and 100 proportional rankings both as they are and with every count ×10¹⁹. It
requires the same committees, winners and lists, and every support and comparison exactly 10¹⁹
times the original. A fault planted in either grouping makes it fail.

Two shortcuts find each contest's groups and patterns faster without changing a figure (29
October 2026). Contests are listed in order, so each is reduced from its parent, the same
members less the last: ballots that don't rank the new member move with their group, and only
those that do are placed one by one (`ContestReductions`). And each contest's patterns are read
for every member in one pass over its groups (`SchulzeStvCounter.completions`).
`ContestReductionsTest` checks the first against reducing each contest afresh, on 300 random
elections with ties and short, blank and complete ballots (over 5,000 contests); it fails when
the groups above a contest merge without counting the members they rank, the one subtle point.
`CompletionsTest` checks the second against the member-by-member patterns on 500 random
reductions, counts past `long` included. Every support of Tideman's 65 elections still matches,
and the JSON reports of `tools/benchmark`'s elections are the same byte for byte. On short
ballots the count takes about half as long (24 candidates for 5 seats, 10,000 voters: 19 s
rather than 29); on complete ones, Tideman's, it's as before, since every ballot ranks every
new member.

## Schulze proportional ranking

The Schulze proportional ranking (`schulze-proportional`, section 9.1 of the paper) builds a
party list place by place: with a1..a(k-1) placed, each remaining b and c are compared by H[b,c]
= N[{a1..a(k-1), b}; c], Schulze STV's support, and the single-winner Schulze method on H fills
place k. Schulze's archive has his program for it (`singl04g.cpp`) and its output (`*_list.dat`)
for Tideman's 66 elections and four Pirate Party list conventions: for each place, the whole of
H to 6 decimals, then who took it.

- **Every comparison.** H depends only on who's already placed, so wherever the two lists agree
  on the places so far, every value of H can be compared; where they part, the election is
  counted again with his list given (`--given-list`), which works out each place's H for his list
  so far, and the rest are compared too. All 170,378 comparisons he prints match, to the 6
  decimals they're given in: Tideman's 66 elections 46,500, and the three Pirate conventions,
  Berlin 2011 (29 candidates, 106 ballots) 8,120, Berlin 2013 (46, 303) 32,430 and Sweden 2014
  (63, 220) 83,328. Before the lists could be followed, only the 80,760 up to where they part
  were checked.
- **The paper's worked example**, A53 (section 9.3): the list J A G D F B E C I H, the fifth place
  going to F over B by the single-winner ranking as the paper says, and all 330 comparisons
  (`SchulzeProportionalTideman53FixtureTest`).
- **The lists.** 57 of the 69 are the program's. The other twelve part where the program isn't
  the paper, and Schulze's own printed H shows why at each: on A04, A12, A23 (at place 3), A35
  and the three Pirate lists (Berlin 2011 at place 4, Berlin 2013 at 10, Sweden 2014 at 7), the
  paper's Stage 3 compares H's links by margin and the program by winning votes, and each picks
  its own candidate; on A13 (at place 13: ours A, his N), A14, A15, A22
  and A33 several candidates go unbeaten either way, and the tie-break decides it: this project uses the single-winner
  Schulze ranking, the program a ranking drawn from a random ballot order. That randomness shows
  in the files themselves: A10, A12, A33, A67 and Sweden 2014 hold two or three runs of the
  program, and they don't all agree. The program also gives the first place to the top of its single-winner
  ranking, where the paper runs Stage 3 for it too; no list parts there.
- **The October 2026 fix to the single-winner ranking** (see [Schulze](#schulze)) changed the
  tie-break ranking of 6 of the lists' elections and one list, A33's. It parts from the program at
  place 5 now (ours F, his H, both among the six candidates unbeaten on his printed H, by margin
  and by winning votes), where it parted at place 8 before (ours F, his N); the comparisons
  checked there fell from 1,608 to 1,210 (to 28,750 in all, before A13 was counted). Every other
  list and comparison is as it was.
- **Supports past the closed form.** For committees of over 8 members, the support, the
  minimum over sets J of f(J) / |J|, is found without the closed form's table over 2^M sets
  (`MinimumNormSupport`). f, the completed weight preferring some member of J, is submodular,
  and Wolfe's minimum-norm-point algorithm, in double precision, finds the point of its base
  polytope nearest the origin: its smallest coordinate bounds the support from below, and the
  members on it are a set whose ratio bounds it from above. When the two meet, to 10^-11 of the
  total weight, the support is computed exactly at that set and its near rivals; otherwise the
  contest is refused (none of Schulze's has been). It is exact unless two sets' ratios come
  within about 10^-11 without being equal. On 600 random contests of up to 16 candidates, short
  ballots and ties of every kind included, every one of over 5,000 supports is the closed
  form's (`MinimumNormSupportTest`), and so are all 510 of A13's, A23's and Berlin 2011's at 21
  to 25 seats; past 25 seats, Schulze's printed H ("Every comparison", above) is the check. It
  replaced a minimum cut over the completion listed out, which ran past its cap of 4 million
  sets on short ballots, and the closed form's 512 MB tables that took over at 21 to 25 seats:
  A13 now takes under 4 s, where it took 2 minutes, and A23 and the two larger Pirate
  conventions, refused before, take 9 s, 2 minutes and 5 minutes.
- **The switch from the closed form at 8 members, not 20** (28 October 2026). On short ballots
  the closed form's table grows slow well before 20: each voter level with the outsider costs a
  pass over the voters for every subset of their tie. Ranked with the switch at 20, 12, 8 and 4
  members, every one of Schulze's 69 lists gives the same JSON report, every comparison
  included, as do `tools/benchmark`'s elections of 16, 20 and 24 candidates; with it at 8, the
  69 take 78 s rather than 546 s (Sweden 2014 19 s rather than 341 s, Berlin 2013 16 s rather
  than 142 s), and 24 candidates ranked by 10,000 voters 5 s rather than 115 s.
- **Completion without listing it out.** The paper defines proportional completion as a chain:
  a voter level with g on T takes a donor's view of T, in proportion to the donors' votes, then
  is completed in turn for what that donor leaves level. Whether the members of a set R all end
  up below g depends only on the donors who say something about R, so the closed form works that
  chance out once per R (`SchulzeStvCounter.Completion`), rather than listing each tie's
  completion over its subsets, which ran to millions for A13's contests and took the count past
  30 minutes. On 300 random contests of up to 7 candidates, ties of every kind and unranked
  candidates included, every one of over 1,000 supports is the one completion listed out as the
  paper defines it gives, in exact fractions (`ProportionalCompletionTest`); a wrong share for a
  tie nobody breaks, or a donor's own preferences left out, fails it. Schulze STV's 315,112
  published supports still all match.

The Berlin Pirates' 2011 list isn't a real outcome to check against: Andreas Baum, who headed
the list the party ran with, is sixth in this ranking of those ballots (and fifth in the
program's), so the convention's list wasn't this method's result on them.

`tools/reference/schulze_stv/proportional_check.py` reruns all of this.

## QPQ

Checked against two independent implementations of Woodall's rule, droop's `qpq.py` and
OpenSTV's `QPQ.py`: six synthetic ballot sets and a purpose-built restart-and-re-election case
match both, quota and every quotient, stage by stage, to 9 decimal places (OpenSTV's transcript
even labels the stage this project marks `restarted` as "Restart count"). Truncated and skipped
tied rankings match both as well.

`tools/reference/qpq` widens that to 1,000 seeded random elections (3–9 candidates, 1–5 seats, a
withdrawn candidate in a quarter of them, weighted and truncated ballots), each counted three
ways. All 1,000 match droop and OpenSTV in every decision, 5,828 in all: each election and
exclusion is the same candidate, at the same quota and quotient to 10⁻⁸ (droop reports 9 places),
including the 168 decisions at a tie (all three break them by candidate order) and the 1,859
after a restart, each a restart in OpenSTV too; and the winners are the same. Only the end differs,
and isn't compared: this project and droop elect the hopefuls left once there are no more than
seats, OpenSTV counts on until they reach the quota. A fault planted in this project's quota
(leaving out the candidates inactive ballots have elected) fails 142 of 200 elections.

Hand-derived cases (`QpqCounterTest`) include the restart-and-re-election mechanic, which caught a
real bug: a final "elect every remaining hopeful" step didn't check whether the seats were already
filled.

The count has two implementations: one on `long` vote counts, and one on `BigInteger` counts for
totals too large for a `long`, which no real election reaches. The external checks above only
exercise the first. `QpqCounterTest` counts 200 random elections (932 stages) both as they are
and with every count ×10¹⁸. It requires the same winners, actions and restarts, and every quota
and quotient exactly 10¹⁸ times the original. Faults planted in either path (ignoring exhausted
credit, ignoring held credit, electing at a quotient equal to the quota, not resetting credit on
a restart) all make it fail.

## Cambridge STV

`CambridgeCounter` follows OpenSTV's `CambridgeSTV` (OpenSTV 1.7,
[Conservatory/openstv](https://github.com/Conservatory/openstv) at commit 2a267a4).

- **Against OpenSTV** (`tools/reference/openstv/cambridge.py`), on 1,000 seeded random elections
  (8–20 candidates on party slates, 3–9 seats, 600–3,000 ballots in a fixed order, some lines of
  weight 2 or 3, a long tail of minor candidates): every stage's counts, the non-transferable
  total and the winners match exactly, through 2,208 surplus transfers, 712 exclusions of several
  candidates at once, and 572 ties OpenSTV reports breaking in 367 of the elections. Two
  deliberate rule mistakes — starting the every-n-th run one ballot late, and not closing a
  candidate who reaches quota mid-transfer — fail 155 and 163 of 200. (The first comparison,
  on 60 generated elections, is what this replaces.)
  `CambridgeOpenStvReferenceTest` replays a stored OpenSTV count stage by stage
  (`testdata/cambridge/README.md` says how it was made); `CambridgeCounterTest` has a
  hand-worked case, confirmed by OpenSTV, where the Cincinnati method gives a whole surplus of
  29 to one candidate that fractional STV would split evenly.
- **Official counts** (`tools/reference/cambridge`): FairVote's collection of proportional RCV
  records (https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/STVUET) has the ballots of all 22 City Council and School
  Committee elections from 2001 to 2023, in the order the City counted them. Against the
  official count sheets the Cambridge Civic Journal keeps of each:
  - **2001–2017, all 16 elections**: every one of the 184 official counts is a round of this
    count, every candidate's votes to the vote, at the official quota, with the same winners,
    and in every one the sheet's exhausted ballots are this count's exhausted votes. The sheets
    are read by the PDF's own coordinates: each table's Total row gives every count's change
    and total, and their centres mark the count's columns, since a count that changes nothing
    may give a row one figure where the others give two. The 2013 Council's sheet holds two
    counts, the original of 15 November and the recount of 2–12 December; the record is the
    original's (its 17,846 ballots, to the recount's 17,851), and all 17 of its counts match,
    where none of the recount's does. The 2005 and 2007 Council sheets add a later vacancy's
    recount (other ballots), left out. (Until October 2026 the checker read the sheets as runs
    of figures, and counted 204: the 2013 recount's 17, a vacancy recount each in 2005 and
    2007, and a misread write-in row in 2015's School Committee.) This count defeats write-ins
    one a round where the City may take them together, so a count's exhausted figure may be
    any of those rounds', in order.
    Which ballots a surplus moves depends on their order, so this checks the Cincinnati
    transfer, starting at the n-th ballot, against the real counts: the same ballots in reverse
    order fail from the first surplus on. The City passes over an overvoted ranking; ending the
    ballot there instead leaves the quota short of the City's in every election. Two are
    fixtures (`CambridgeOfficialCountTest`: the 2017 Council's 19 counts, the 2013 School
    Committee's 8).
  - **2019–2023, 6 elections**: the winners only. These records don't mark overvotes (each is a
    plain "skipped"), so the first counts are a few votes a candidate off the City's, the 2023
    Council's quota 2,345 to the City's 2,334, and no count can match. Five elect the City's
    winners. The 2023 School Committee doesn't: the City's last exclusion was King's, 8 votes
    behind Harding (2,624 to 2,632), and here it's Harding, 12 behind King, within what the
    missing overvotes move.
  - The records' write-ins aren't always the City's (2019 and 2023 lump them as one); every
    write-in is under 50 votes and goes in the first exclusion either way. The 2023 School
    Committee record names its last two rankings by code ("C06[10]"); the converter works out
    which candidate each stands for from the ballots that use it.
- **Ballot order** (`tools/order-sensitivity`): at 10,000 voters, 200 random orders of each of
  30 generated elections never changed the result. In smaller electorates with many seats it
  sometimes does, and at 1,000 ballots for 9 seats — a quota near the 50-vote cutoff — it can
  decide whether every seat is filled (53, 131 and 193 of 200 orders in three such elections,
  counted the same way by OpenSTV).

## Changes checked for identical results

Performance work replaced several engines' internals. Each change was checked against the
previous implementation on random elections — every result identical — with a deliberate
mistake confirming each check could fail: classic STV (2,000 elections, every quota, transfer,
rounding, batch exclusion, tied-ranking policy and minimum-preferences setting), QPQ (2,000),
CPO-STV (400, every pairwise score), Schulze (1,500, both matrices), the BLT parser (164 files),
Meek/Warren (byte-identical output at 100,000–1,000,000 voters, and droop on a
100,000-voter count), and Schulze STV (every exact support, the potential winners and winners
on Tideman's 65 elections and 300 random ones with ties and short ballots, plus byte-identical
JSON on three 10,000-voter synthetic counts). A full JVM-level audit of iteration-order dependence (66 method/election
pairs, 10 fresh JVM runs each) found the CPO-STV bug above and nothing else.

(other readings tried, the record shown to differ); two aren't shown, and are listed last.
